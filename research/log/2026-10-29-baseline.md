## Hypothesis

None. This entry records the state of the benchmark at the moment the research
loop was set up, so later entries have something to compare against, and it
reports what the first measurements already say about where time goes.

## What was done

Blake was extracted from Lean 4 v4.35.0 with Lake's sources unchanged (one
two-line fix for `lake lint`/`lake shake` search paths when `lake` is not next to
`lean`). The harness measured stock Lake, Blake `main`, and the candidate on the
three medium corpora (1000 modules each: layered, deep, wide). `main` and the
candidate are two builds of the same Lake source, so their comparison is the
noise floor of the harness itself, and that comparison was used to calibrate the
measurement: tools are interleaved run by run (measured one after another, three
equivalent binaries drifted from 0.18 to 0.23 s on a no-op build); sub-second
scenarios are run 10 times and compared on their minima with a 7% threshold
(their timings are bimodal, ~20% apart, which defeats medians); longer scenarios
use medians with `max(3%, spread/median)`; the single-sample `cold` uses 7%.

## Conclusion

- **Blake is Lake.** Cold, `swarm`, and `hub` times match stock Lake within noise
  and every artifact is byte-identical after every build. The leaderboard starts
  at roughly 1.0x.
- **The one systematic difference is startup.** Blake's `noop` is about 0.03-0.05 s
  slower than stock Lake's (0.20-0.22 s vs 0.17-0.18 s) on every corpus, and
  `leaf` inherits the same gap. Two candidate causes, both unverified: Blake is not
  installed next to `lean`, so each invocation spawns `lean --print-prefix`
  (`Lake/Config/InstallPath.lean`, ~30 ms here); and Blake's binary is a 240 MB
  statically linked executable built with `supportInterpreter`, whereas stock
  `lake` is a small dynamically linked one. The first iteration should measure
  which it is before fixing either.
- **The cost structure of an edit.** A no-op build of 1000 modules costs ~0.17 s;
  one leaf edit ~0.4-0.6 s, of which ~0.1 s is `lean` process startup and the
  rest elaboration of that one module; a `hub` edit costs the same as a cold
  build (~33 s) because every transitive dependent is rebuilt even though the
  appended theorem changes no downstream artifact. Finer-grained invalidation
  (rebuild a dependent only when something it uses changed) is therefore the
  largest lever on `hub` and `swarm`; the breaking-edit check exists to keep such
  a scheme honest.
- **Noise.** With identical binaries measured sequentially, cold builds differed
  by up to ~5%, `hub` and `swarm` by ~1-2%, and sub-second scenarios by up to
  ~15%; interleaving and minima bring the sub-second disagreement to a few
  percent. Treat any sub-second improvement under 7% and any single-sample cold
  improvement under 7% as noise; the harness does.
- Next: profile a `noop` build (`strace -c`, `perf`) to split Blake's 0.2 s into
  process startup, workspace loading, and per-module trace checks, then attack the
  largest piece. Separately, instrument how the `noop` cost scales with module
  count using the `large-*` configs (8000 modules).
